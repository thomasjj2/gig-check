How Gig Check Works

Gig Check helps DoorDash drivers decide whether an offer is worth accepting. Enter a job's payout and total miles by hand, or tap "Check This Order" and upload a screenshot of the offer — Gig Check reads the payout and mileage from the image automatically. Using your vehicle's MPG and current gas price, it calculates your estimated gas cost, profit after gas, profit per mile, and estimated hourly rate, then shows a Skip, Maybe, or Take recommendation based on the minimum profit per mile you set. Jobs you take are logged in your History tab, which tracks fuel spend, profit after fuel, and an estimated tax deduction using the IRS standard mileage rate — exportable as a CSV mileage log at tax time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a DoorDash order is worth taking?

Enter the payout and total miles for the offer into Gig Check, or upload a screenshot of the offer and let it read the numbers automatically. Gig Check calculates your estimated gas cost, profit after gas, profit per mile, and estimated hourly rate, then gives you a Skip, Maybe, or Take recommendation based on your own target profit per mile.

What counts as a good profit per mile for gig delivery?

It depends on your vehicle and the cost of gas, which is why Gig Check lets you set your own minimum profit-per-mile target rather than using a fixed number. Many drivers use somewhere between $1 and $2 profit per mile after gas as a starting point, then adjust based on their own vehicle's fuel efficiency and expenses.

Does Gig Check work with gig apps other than DoorDash?

Right now, Gig Check's screenshot scanner is built and tested specifically for DoorDash offers. You can still use the manual payout/miles entry fields for any gig platform, and screenshot support for other apps is planned.
